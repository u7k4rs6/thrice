**C1: the plan is Starter, not free.** $20 monthly credits, 20 concurrent browsers, 2 concurrent sandboxes, 5 hour maximum session, 7 day replay retention, stealth available. Consequences worked through in sections 4, 7, 8, and in `02-architecture.md` sections 4 and 7.

## 1. Summary

thrice takes a GitHub issue for a self-hosted open source web app, forks a Solari sandbox from a prepared snapshot of that app at a pinned version, seeds known backend state, runs the reported reproduction steps in three Solari browsers, checks concrete predicates, and produces one of four verdicts: REPRODUCED (3 of 3, with video), FLAKY (1 or 2 of 3, with a first-divergence diff), NOT_REPRODUCED (0 of 3 with every step completing), or INCONCLUSIVE (with a machine-readable reason).

v1 does not post anything anywhere. It runs against a corpus of already-fixed issues, where each entry is a triple of (issue, buggy version, fixed version) and the correct answer is known in advance: REPRODUCED on the buggy version and NOT_REPRODUCED on the fixed one. That turns the deliverable into a measurement of thrice itself. The target is exactly one app: jaeger-ui, served by the Jaeger 2.x all-in-one binary. thrice is reliability engineering applied to bug triage: the claim it makes is not "an agent reproduced this" but "this predicate held three times out of three on this version, and here is the evidence".

> *Defect record, C2.* Previously: "A human approves before any verdict reaches an issue comment." No verdict reaches an issue comment in v1. The approval flow is specified and unbuilt; see section 6.

## 2. Problem

A maintainer reading an open issue cannot cheaply answer three questions: does this still reproduce, is it deterministic or flaky, and on which version. Answering by hand means building the app, seeding matching data, and clicking through the steps: ten to forty minutes per issue. So issues sit, and the ones that sit longest are the ones where the reporter's environment is least clear.

An agent that drives a browser and reports "yes, I reproduced it" is an unreliable narrator twice over: it decides what the steps meant, and it decides whether the outcome matched. Both decisions are unobservable and non-deterministic across runs, and a wrong answer spends maintainer trust.

thrice separates the two. An LLM turns prose into a structured plan once, offline, and a human reviews it before it runs. The run is deterministic and keyless: a fixed step list, typed predicates evaluated against the DOM, console, and network. Three runs, because one cannot distinguish a bug from a race, and flakiness is the finding maintainers most want and least often get. Two predicates, because "the buggy thing happened" and "the correct thing did not happen" are different claims and a bug report needs both (D4).

The tool that makes that claim has to be measured before anyone should believe it. That is what the corpus is for.

## 3. Users

The first readers are Pinetree's reviewer and OSS maintainers evaluating whether the tool is worth their attention. They reach it through the public repo and the post, not through a comment on an issue they own. What they need, in order: the corpus result (how often thrice is right when the answer is known), the verdict taxonomy, the cost per attempt, and one worked example they can read end to end in a few minutes.

Usage is no longer generated by posting. The adoption unit is a reader of the repo, so the repo and the report pages carry the whole argument.

Secondary, explicitly not v1: OSS maintainers adding thrice as a GitHub Action with their own Solari key, shaped by the access model in `03-security-and-access.md` section 5. Nothing in v1 should foreclose it.

> *Defect record, C2.* Previously: "The first ten users are jaeger-ui maintainers and contributors reading a verdict comment on an issue they already care about ... the adoption unit is a comment, not a download."

## 4. The reviewer as a stakeholder

The bar is technical merit plus whether the project makes Solari look good, so Solari primitives must be load-bearing rather than decorative. **Sandboxes** run the target app, not a toy script. **Snapshots** are why an attempt costs seconds instead of minutes (D1), and under C2 they are also what makes multi-version work possible at all: the corpus needs a snapshot per Jaeger version, which is gate G7. **previewUrl** makes the sandboxed app reachable and is the single point of failure the day-1 gates exist to test (G1). **Browsers** run three per attempt. **Session recording** or a CDP screencast produces the video (G3).

Under C1 the concurrency story changes and is worth stating precisely, because the obvious reading of "Starter unlocks fork-per-run" is not quite right. Starter allows 20 concurrent browsers and **2** concurrent sandboxes. Three runs therefore cannot each get their own sandbox simultaneously: fork-per-run is now affordable and architecturally available, but it executes as two waves (two runs, then one) rather than three in parallel. Browsers stop being a constraint entirely at 20; the sandbox cap of 2 becomes the thing that serialises work. See section 8 and `02-architecture.md` section 7.

> *Defect record, C1.* Previously: "**Browsers** run three at once, exactly the free-tier limit, so the design visibly respects the platform's constraints instead of assuming a bigger plan." Three browsers is now a property of the method (three runs per attempt), not of the plan.

## 5. Scope and non-goals

v1 scope: one app (jaeger-ui on the Jaeger 2.x all-in-one binary), one seeding mechanism (OTLP/HTTP JSON to localhost:4318 inside the sandbox, D2), one plan source (schema-validated JSON, D3), three runs per attempt, four verdicts, a corpus of already-fixed issues with known ground truth, and a corpus scoring report.

Non-goals, verbatim from the brief: arbitrary repos, a leaderboard, named commercial agents, third-party websites, and a hosted web service. Added under C2: **posting to any public issue tracker**, and any contact with maintainers about running thrice on their backlog. Also out of v1: opening pull requests (never), LLM repair of a failed locator (v2, D3), full DOM diffing (D6), and any target whose UI mutates backend state during a run.

## 6. Verdict taxonomy and corpus scoring

The taxonomy is unchanged (D5):

| Verdict | Condition |
|---|---|
| REPRODUCED | 3 of 3: actual predicate holds and expected predicate fails |
| FLAKY | 1 or 2 of 3, with a first-divergence diff |
| NOT_REPRODUCED | 0 of 3, every step completing in every run |
| INCONCLUSIVE | Any run did not complete (locator_miss, timeout, 429, session cap, seed failure) |

**Corpus scoring.** A corpus entry is a triple `(issue, buggy_version, fixed_version)`, where the fixed version is the first Jaeger release containing the commit that closed the issue. One entry costs two attempts, one per version. thrice is **correct on that entry only if it returns REPRODUCED on `buggy_version` and NOT_REPRODUCED on `fixed_version`**. Any other combination is incorrect, including FLAKY on either side.

The report gives per-entry outcomes and an aggregate of correct, incorrect, and INCONCLUSIVE. **INCONCLUSIVE is reported separately and never counted as correct.** An entry where either attempt came back INCONCLUSIVE is scored as inconclusive for the entry, not silently dropped, because dropping it would flatter the number.

Per-attempt, the two versions give a confusion matrix, so precision and recall are well defined:

| | REPRODUCED | NOT_REPRODUCED |
|---|---|---|
| **on buggy_version** | true positive | false negative |
| **on fixed_version** | false positive | true negative |

Precision is TP / (TP + FP): when thrice says a bug reproduces, how often is there really a bug. Recall is TP / (TP + FN): of the bugs that are really there, how many thrice finds. FLAKY and INCONCLUSIVE are excluded from both and reported as their own rates, because folding them into either would make the headline number mean less than it appears to.

## 7. Success criteria for day 7

| Criterion | Target | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Corpus entries built and run | **6** (floor 5, no stretch) | Bounded by the release window, not by plan-authoring time and not by predicate expressiveness. See below. |
| Attempts executed | 12 (two per entry) | One per version per entry. |
| Corpus correctness reported with precision and recall | Yes, whatever the number is | The result is the deliverable. A low number is a finding. |
| INCONCLUSIVE rate reported separately | Yes | Never folded into correctness. |
| Recording published | 1, 30 to 45 seconds | The reviewer's primary artifact. |
| Repo public with report pages for every attempt | Yes | 12 report pages are the evidence. |
| Cookbook fork with `examples/thrice-jaeger` | Yes | Satisfies the literal fork requirement. |
| Credits spent for the week | Under $3.00 of $20.00 | Section 8. |

**Why 6 entries, and why there is no stretch figure.** Day-2 triage (`docs/corpus-candidates.md`) surveyed all 146 jaeger-ui issues closed in the window, took the 44 labelled `bug` and completed, and found 7 that qualified. Day 5 excluded #4131 on closer reading of its observable, leaving **6**.

> *Defect record, day 5.* Was 7. #4131's only observable is scroll position in a virtualized list, which needs a 40-span trace to detect, failing C1 and C3. The count moved down rather than up, which is what section 7 predicted it would do. The ceiling is not plan-authoring time and it is not predicate expressiveness. **It is the 2.14.0 to 2.20.0 release window** that gate G7 established: an entry needs both a buggy and a fixed release inside it, and most candidates do not have one.

A stretch figure would be dishonest here. There is no eighth entry to reach for: the pool is exhausted, and getting to 8 would mean promoting a MARGINAL candidate, which is exactly what the floor of 5 exists to prevent. If the number moves it will move down, when an entry turns out not to reproduce as triaged, not up.

**Exclusion breakdown, reportable as a result.** This is a finding about the method, not bookkeeping, and it belongs in the README on day 7. Of 44 candidates, 35 were excluded, and **22 of those 35 failed on release-pair availability alone (criterion C5)**, not on anything to do with what thrice can express:

| Exclusion cause | Count | Note |
|---|---|---|

| C5, no identifiable fix commit | 11 | The referenced PR was closed unmerged, so ground truth is unavailable |

The honest reading: for two thirds of the excluded issues thrice could have produced a verdict, and the obstacle was that the versions needed to check it against do not exist as buildable releases. Snapshots needed: **five, not fourteen**. The 7 entries cluster onto four adjacent release pairs (v2.16.0 to v2.17.0, v2.17.0 to v2.18.0, v2.18.0 to v2.19.0, v2.19.0 to v2.20.0), so the corpus needs snapshots for v2.16.0, v2.17.0, v2.18.0, v2.19.0 and v2.20.0 only.

## 8. Budget model

**Starter rates**, verified from docs.getsolari.com on 2026-09-02: browsers $0.10 per hour, 20 concurrent; sandboxes $0.035 per vCPU-hour plus $0.011 per GB-hour, 2 concurrent; $20 monthly credits; 5 hour maximum session; 7 day replay retention.

- Browser: $0.10 / 3600 = **$0.00002778 per browser-second**.
- Sandbox at 1 vCPU / 2 GB: (1 x $0.035) + (2 x $0.011) = $0.035 + $0.022 = $0.057 per hour, and $0.057 / 3600 = **$0.00001583 per sandbox-second**. (This matches the published table exactly, unlike the free-tier figure which the table rounded.)

Two execution models are now available. Both are costed, because C1 makes the second viable and the choice is no longer forced.

**Model A, shared instance** (one sandbox, three browsers against it in parallel, as originally designed):

| Item | Quantity | Arithmetic | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 browsers at about 120 s | 360 browser-seconds | 360 x $0.00002778 | $0.0100 |
| 1 sandbox: fork, seed, health, hold, kill | 180 sandbox-seconds | 180 x $0.00001583 | $0.0029 |
| **Total** | | | **$0.0129** |

**Model B, fork-per-run** (one sandbox per run, executed as two waves because the Starter sandbox cap is 2):

| Item | Quantity | Arithmetic | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 browsers at about 120 s | 360 browser-seconds | 360 x $0.00002778 | $0.0100 |
| 3 sandboxes at about 170 s each (fork, seed, health, run, kill) | 510 sandbox-seconds | 510 x $0.00001583 | $0.0081 |
| **Total** | | | **$0.0181** |

Model B costs 40% more per attempt ($0.0181 against $0.0129, a difference of $0.0052) and roughly doubles wall clock, because two waves. It buys complete run independence: no shared backend state, so a target whose UI mutates state is no longer excluded by construction. For jaeger-ui, which is read-only over seeded data, Model A remains the default and Model B is available per plan. See `02-architecture.md` section 4.

**Ceiling per attempt** (D7), at the 10 minute per-run timeout, computed on Model B as the more expensive case:

| Item | Quantity | Arithmetic | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 browsers at 600 s | 1800 browser-seconds | 1800 x $0.00002778 | $0.0500 |
| 3 sandboxes at 900 s | 2700 sandbox-seconds | 2700 x $0.00001583 | $0.0427 |
| **Ceiling, enforced as $0.10** | | | **$0.0927** |

**Day-7 corpus total.** 8 entries x 2 attempts = 16 attempts. At Model A: 16 x $0.0129 = **$0.206**. At Model B: 16 x $0.0181 = **$0.290**. Snapshot builds, 12 versions at about 300 sandbox-seconds each: 12 x 300 x $0.00001583 = **$0.057**. Development reruns and failures, assume three times the corpus figure: about **$0.87**. Day-1 gates, roughly 25 sandbox creates and 15 browser launches: about **$0.20**. Week total about **$1.40 of $20.00**, and under $3.00 even if every attempt runs to its ceiling.

Monthly capacity for context: $20.00 / $0.0181 = **about 1,100 attempts**, or 550 corpus entries. Budget is not a constraint on this project at Starter under any plausible plan. The constraints are 2 concurrent sandboxes and human plan-authoring time.

> *Defect record, C1.* The free-tier model this replaces: browser $0.00004167/s, sandbox $0.00002375/s, $0.0193 per attempt, ceiling $0.0964, about 155 attempts per month on $3.00, with 1 concurrent sandbox and 3 concurrent browsers. The old note that "the binding constraint on free tier is not money, it is 1 concurrent sandbox, 3 concurrent browsers, and the 1 hour session cap" is still directionally true at Starter: money matters even less, and the sandbox cap is still what serialises attempts, just at 2 rather than 1.

## 9. Seven-day plan

**Day 1: gates only. No feature work.** The gates can invalidate the architecture, and under C2 the corpus adds one that did not exist before.

- **G1** previewUrl returns a reachable public URL, and a Solari browser can load it. Also: 425 Too Early behaviour and time to resolve, and whether the `pt_token` is visible in `page.url` from the browser side, since `03-security-and-access.md`'s redaction control depends on the answer.
- **G5** Sandbox egress to github.com for the release tarball, with the extracted binaries verified against the project's published `sha256sum.txt`.
- **G2** snapshot, then kill, then create(from_snapshot) works; measure fork time; confirm Jaeger is healthy after the fork; determine whether a forked store carries seeded traces or comes up empty; measure `revert(snapshot_id)`.
- **G7 (new, C2)** Multi-version feasibility. Two different Jaeger release tarballs build into two separate snapshots and a fork from each is healthy. Per-version snapshot build time. If pinning older 2.x releases is a problem (missing assets, changed layout, different binary names) that has to surface now, because the corpus design rests on it.
- **G3** A video path exists. All three options were measured to work; ranking is on merit: screencast first, `record_video` second, session replay third. See `02-architecture.md` section 8.
- **G4** Python SDK parity for previewUrl and snapshot. Answered on day 0 by G6.
- **G6** The solari-sandbox 0.2.0 Python surface, verified by reading shipped source. Answered in `02-architecture.md` section 4; it required no credits.

Order: G1, G5, G2, G7, G3, stopping at the first hard failure. If G1 fails, stop and report rather than implementing a fallback.

**Gate kill criteria.** G1 failing stops day 1. G2 failing means every attempt pays the full build cost, so the corpus drops to 4 entries and the reason is stated. G7 failing means the corpus cannot span versions, which is fatal to the C2 design and forces a return to single-version work with a different day-7 artifact. G3 failing on all three options means the recording shows the terminal and report pages instead of video. G5 failing means uploading the tarball via `files.write`: slower, not blocking.

> *Defect record, C2.* Deleted from this section: the day-1 instruction to message the jaeger-ui lead maintainer, and the day-4 kill criterion that made posting conditional on his reply. No maintainer is contacted. Q2 is closed as withdrawn.

> *Defect record, C1.* Deleted from this section: the G1 fallback to a free VPS. It was a free-tier contingency for the case where no preview domain exists; the instruction for day 1 is now to stop and report if G1 fails rather than to route around it.

**Days 2 to 3:** plan schema and validator, manual planner template, environment manager (multi-version snapshot build, fork, seed, health, kill), budget guard. First entry run on both versions.

**Days 4 to 5:** runner with the browser semaphore, predicate evaluator, trajectory recorder, divergence differ, verdict engine. Corpus authoring in parallel.

**Day 6:** reporter (JSON, static HTML per attempt, corpus summary page), remaining corpus entries, corpus scoring.

**Day 7:** recording, post, cookbook fork, cleanup, no new features. Day 7 is reserved entirely, because a recording made in a hurry is the one artifact the reviewer definitely sees.

## 10. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| previewUrl unavailable (G1) | Tested first on day 1. Under C1 there is no fallback: G1 failing stops the day and the design is reconsidered rather than routed around. |
| Preview URL expiry mid-attempt | The signed URL carries a one-hour `pt_token`. Per-run timeout is 10 minutes and the URL is fetched per attempt, so expiry cannot bite inside a run. A 401 is a harness bug, not a verdict. |
| Old Jaeger releases cannot be pinned (G7) | Fatal to the corpus design, which is exactly why it is a day-1 gate rather than a day-4 discovery. |
| No video path works (G3) | Three ranked options, reordered on evidence. Worst case the report page carries screenshot timelines and the recording shows the terminal plus report pages. |
| **Low corpus correctness** | Under C2 this changes shape entirely and is worth being explicit about. Previously, a poor verdict distribution was a public claim about somebody's open bug, where being wrong cost a maintainer's trust and there was no ground truth to check against. Now the answer is known in advance for every entry, so a low score is a **measured limitation of thrice**, reported with precision, recall, and a per-entry breakdown of what went wrong. That is a result, not an embarrassment, and it is the main reason C2 is an improvement rather than a retreat: the project can now be wrong in public without anyone else paying for it. A corpus where thrice is right on 5 of 8 entries with the three failures explained is a better artifact than three unverifiable comments on live issues. |
| Corpus selection bias | Entries are chosen before running, recorded in the repo with the fixing commit, and never removed after seeing a result. Removing a losing entry after the fact is the one thing that would make the number worthless. |
| Solari outage during the build week | The runner is testable against recorded CDP and HTTP fixtures at zero credits (`02-architecture.md` section 12), so an outage blocks gates and attempts but not development. |
| Budget exhaustion | Expected week spend about $1.40 against $20.00. The guard refuses to start a run that would cross a ceiling, and a 429 is treated as a guard bug rather than retried. |
| Non-retryable 429 from concurrency overrun | Local semaphore of 2 sandboxes and 3 browsers enforced before any API call, plus idempotency keys on create routes so a retried create cannot double-charge. |
| Jaeger version drift | Pinned by digest per corpus entry, and printed on every report page. |

> *Defect record, C2.* Deleted risk row: "Maintainer annoyance. Day-1 invitation with a day-4 kill criterion, one comment per issue ever, AI disclosure in every comment, and a withdrawal procedure." No comments are made, so the risk does not arise.
